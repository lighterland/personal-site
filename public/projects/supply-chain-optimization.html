Strategic Analysis: Optimizing Global Supply Chain Operations

A comprehensive deep dive into reducing late delivery rates from 57% to 19% using the DataCo Smart Supply Chain dataset.

Portfolio Author
Data Analyst & Supply Chain Strategist

1. Executive Summary

In the competitive landscape of global retail, delivery reliability is a key driver of customer retention. Analysis of the DataCo Smart Supply Chain Dataset reveals a critical inefficiency: between 2015 and 2017, the organization suffered a 57% late delivery rate. This operational failure correlated directly with a significant 15% decline in year-over-year transactions in 2017, signaling a potential loss of customer trust.

This report outlines the analytical methodology used to diagnose the root causes of these delays and proposes a data-backed roadmap to achieve a target late delivery rate of 19% by the end of FY 2018.

2. Data Overview & Methodology

The analysis utilized the comprehensive DataCo dataset, which encompasses detailed records across Provisioning, Production, Sales, and Commercial Distribution. The dataset includes over 180,000 transactions, providing a robust foundation for identifying systemic patterns.

  • Tools Utilized: SQL for data extraction and aggregation; Python (Pandas) for statistical analysis; Tableau/Chart.js for visualization.
  • Key Metrics Tracked: Days for shipping (Scheduled vs. Real), Delivery Status, Order Status, and Shipping Mode.
  • Scope: Global supply chain operations from 2015 to 2017.

3. Root Cause Diagnosis

The investigation isolated three primary bottlenecks contributing to the 57% delay rate:

A. The Inventory Paradox

Despite consistent demand in Q1-Q3, the analysis revealed a severe stockout crisis. Only 17% of orders had products physically available in stock ("Ready Stock") at the time of purchase. The remaining 83% of orders triggered backorders or on-demand production, adding unavoidable latency to the fulfillment process. This single factor accounted for nearly half (46%) of all observed delays.

B. Shipping Mode Misalignment

Logistics data highlighted a strategic failure in shipping mode selection. 93% of all shipments utilized "Standard Class" shipping (2-4 days transit), while only 7% utilized "Same Day" or "First Class" options.

When inventory is unavailable (Process A), relying on the slowest shipping method (Process B) compounds the delay, making on-time delivery mathematically impossible for many orders.

C. Planning & Scheduling Errors

Approximately 11% of shipments that were physically capable of arriving on time were still marked as late due to internal planning errors. This suggests a disconnect between the Production Planning and Inventory Control (PPIC) team's estimates and the logistics reality.

4. Strategic Recommendations

To reverse the downward trend in transactions and improve reliability, the following three-pillar strategy is recommended:

Recommendation 1: Forecast-Based Inventory Loading

Action: Shift from reactive production to predictive stocking for the Top 10 High-Volume Subcategories.
Goal: Increase "Ready Stock" availability from 17% to 46%.
Method: Implement a "Forecast-per-Month" logic that buffers inventory ahead of identified seasonal peaks (Q1-Q3).

Recommendation 2: Dynamic Logistics Upgrade

Action: Automated shipping upgrades.
Logic: If Stock Status = "Unavailable", automatically upgrade Shipping Mode to "Same Day" or "First Class".
Impact: The faster transit time compensates for the production delay, preserving the customer promise date.

5. Conclusion

The 15% drop in sales is a lagging indicator of the supply chain's reliability issues. By addressing the root cause—inventory availability—and creating a logistics safety net, the organization can realistically reduce late deliveries to 19% within one fiscal year. This recovery in service levels is projected to stabilize transaction volume and restore customer confidence.
